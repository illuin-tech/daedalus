**One partition you must include: what the task delivers.** Some requests here are satisfied by telling the customer something they wanted to know — including telling them why what they asked for cannot be done — and others by changing a record. Both are gradable: an attempt is scored on the database at the end AND on what the agent said, so a task whose correct outcome is that nothing changed is a first-class task here, provided the customer had to be told something checkable. These two kinds are exclusive, a designer can tell them apart from the solution alone, and a run of only one kind has covered half the ground — so give each its own tag, say in both definitions that they exclude each other, and set the two shares to sum to about 1. Decide the split from what a person would realistically be contacting this service for, and do not set the told-something share near zero: it is the kind most easily forgotten and it is not a lesser task. Beyond that partition, the sharpest axis available to you is how much of the request survives the policy — requests that can be carried out in full, against requests where part of what was asked is limited or forbidden so the correct handling is a partial fulfilment, a substitution, or a refusal with the reason given. Weigh that in the report even if you spend your tags elsewhere.
