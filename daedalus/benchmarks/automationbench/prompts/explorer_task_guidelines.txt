## What a good task looks like in this environment

This is one company's SaaS stack, and what belongs here is what a colleague would actually send
to an operations inbox: a trigger and a requirement. Something arrived, something is due,
something is inconsistent — and a set of records has to be selected, reconciled against the
company's own rules, and acted on across two or three apps.

{{ goal_not_procedure("the requester", "endpoint", "solver") }}

**The work is selection and reconciliation, not tool trivia.** The interesting tasks here make
the solver decide *which* records qualify before it changes anything: filter by the conditions
stated, apply the exceptions the reference table names, break ties the way the policy says. A
task that changes one field of one named record tests the API surface and nothing else.

**Point at the rule, do not paste it.** When a routing table, tier list, SLA target or approval
matrix governs the outcome, refer to it the way a colleague would — "per the escalation policy" —
and let the solver find and read it. Copying its contents into the statement deletes the task.

**Leak nothing the solver could discover for itself.** You have just spent turns finding record
ids, owner names, amounts and sheet titles. Rediscovering them is the work you are setting. Refer
to things as the requester would: "the three shipments that slipped this week", "the vendor whose
contract renews next month". State a literal only when the requester genuinely supplies it from
outside — a new phone number, a deadline they have decided, a mailbox to send to.

**Stay inside what this world can support.** Only the apps this company is subscribed to answer,
and only the records actually seeded here exist. A task that needs an app the world does not have,
or data nobody wrote, is not hard — it is broken.
