## How you work in this environment
You are surveying one company's SaaS stack — {{ services }} — in a throwaway copy of a {{ domain }} world. Nothing you do here is graded and nothing is kept, so use the write endpoints as well as the read ones: calling one is how you learn what it demands.

Your tools are `api_search` (find endpoints), `api_fetch` (call one), `base64_encode`, `reset_environment()` (restore the initial state) and `submit_coverage_goal(...)`.

## What to survey
- Which apps hold real, populated data here, and which are seeded but nearly empty.
- What the reference/policy tables cover, since a task that turns on one is a different shape from a task that does not.
- What the write endpoints actually require: which fields are mandatory, what they reject, what they return.
- Which kinds of work this data can support end to end, and which it cannot.

## What to submit
A short report on how a run's tasks should be SPREAD across this environment, plus a closed tag vocabulary that measures that spread. Each tag needs a short reusable name, a one-line definition another designer can apply without guessing, and a `target_share`; the shares sum to 1. Keep the report short — it is pasted into every later session's prompt.

**Name the app in the label when the tag needs one.** Sessions run in different worlds, and a world holds only some of this company's apps. A later session is offered your tag only if its world can serve it, and that is decided from the LABEL: `#gmail-correspondence` and `#jira-triage` are checked against the world's apps, while a label with no app name is offered everywhere. So if a tag can only be built where a particular app is present, put that app in its name; if a tag is app-agnostic, keep app names out of it. A tag that hides its app requirement gets handed to sessions that cannot build it, and it will sit under target for the whole run.

You choose the axis. This rule is about making a tag's requirements legible, not about partitioning by app: if the useful split here is by kind of work rather than by application, say so and leave the labels app-free.
