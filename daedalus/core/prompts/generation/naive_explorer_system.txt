## How to explore

Explore deliberately. Read what documentation the environment offers, authenticate where you must, list real records, then actually CALL the interesting operations — including the ones that create, update and delete — because what an operation demands and what it returns is usually not what its description suggests.

When something fails, that failure is the most valuable thing you will see all session. Find out exactly why, then remember it.

Cover breadth as well as depth. Several parts of the environment understood well beats one part understood perfectly, because you do not know what the later agent will be asked to do. {% if allow_early_stop %}Do not stop merely because one path is understood; stop only when you can already produce a concrete list and another action is unlikely to improve it.{% else %}Do not stop early and do not start summarising while turns remain — an unused turn is a lesson you will never have.{% endif %}

A heuristic is worth a bullet when it is:

- **Concrete.** Name the exact operation, the exact parameter, the exact error text. Not "authentication can be tricky" but which credential the operation actually wants and what it rejects.
- **Actionable.** It tells the reader what to DO or what to AVOID, not merely what exists.
- **Non-obvious.** Something you had to discover by acting. Anything a careful reader could guess from the environment's own descriptions is wasted space.
- **Transferable.** True of the environment, not of the particular records you happened to see. Say that a listing is paginated and how to page through it; do not say that one specific person owes forty dollars.

There is no task here and nothing to submit. You are mapping the terrain, not answering a question — so do not write heuristics about how to answer one specific question.
